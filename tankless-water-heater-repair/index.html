A tankless water heater keeps no reserve of hot water. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is efficient and space-saving, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In Salisbury, MD, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

Our approach to tankless repair is simple: measure first, replace second. When we arrive at your Salisbury home, we record the active code and the unit's fault history, then work through the systems behind it. Flow sensors are checked with water running to confirm they report demand correctly. Flame rods and igniters are tested electrically. Gas pressure is read at the inlet with the burner at maximum, because a line that holds steady at idle can sag badly under full load. The vent and air intake are inspected end to end, and the heat exchanger is judged by the temperature rise it achieves at a known flow. Only then do we recommend a fix, and we can show you exactly why.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. Scale can be flushed out, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. We carry common parts for the major brands and service both gas and electric units, so the typical repair is done in one trip. We are also honest about the harder cases: a unit fed by a gas line too small for its full BTU rating will keep throwing codes until the line is upsized, and a heat exchanger damaged by scale may not be worth repairing on an older heater. And if your complaint is the cold-water sandwich, that brief burst of cold between two hot draws, we will tell you whether it is normal for your model or something we can reduce. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Unusual noises from the cabinet, like a whine, a roar, or a rattle
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before
  • Moisture, rust, or drips appearing below the heater
  • A fault code on the front panel and no hot water
  • A heater that only fires when water runs at full blast
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Consider the risks first. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. On the electric side, the loads are large, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

Professional service is usually the cheaper path, too. Our technicians do not guess at error codes. They test flow sensors with water moving, measure flame rod signal, confirm igniter resistance, read inlet gas pressure at full fire, and check the vent from end to end. When scale is the problem, we flush it out properly and rinse the system before the unit goes back in service. And because some tankless problems, like an undersized gas line or a cracked heat exchanger, are not solved by parts at all, we tell you so directly, before you commit to anything.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Here's where to begin:

  • Flush scale out of the heat exchanger annually, and sooner in hard water
  • Check the inlet filter screen periodically, since a clogged one starves the unit of flow
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
  • Keep the vent termination and air intake clear of leaves, nests, snow, and lint
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
