Many of the installation calls we get in Salisbury, MD are not replacements at all. A family adds a bathroom and needs more capacity. A homeowner wants the heater out of a hall closet and into the garage or basement. A remodel needs a second heater on the far side of the house, or a move to all-electric swaps a gas unit for a heat pump model. What these jobs share is new work: gas lines that must be sized for the load, vents that must be routed and pitched correctly, circuits that must match the heater, and water lines that must be run cleanly. Salisbury Water Heater works all of that out before the installation starts.

Before any pipe is cut, we size the heater to your household. Tanks are compared by first-hour rating, not just gallons, and tankless units are sized by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your water needs in winter. Then we pick the location with you. Gas models require a vent route outdoors and adequate combustion air. A heat pump model has to have breathing room, temperatures above roughly 40 degrees, and a condensate drain. An electric tankless model often has to have its own high-amperage circuit. Anywhere a leak could ruin finished space, a drain pan goes underneath. You get clear choices and a firm price before work begins.

The install itself follows a checklist: gas piping sized, run, fitted with a sediment trap, and leak-tested, vents built to the manufacturer's size and pitch, circuits and breakers matched to the heater's load, and water lines connected with shutoffs and dielectric protection between copper and steel. The relief valve gets a full-size discharge line to a safe point, and we add an expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps wherever code calls for them, including the permit when one is required. To finish, we fill and purge the tank, test draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, set the thermostat near 120 degrees, and walk you through the new setup. Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Taps at the far end of the house that take minutes to warm up
  • A tank that cannot keep up with your household anymore
  • Big-tub or multi-shower demand the current heater cannot meet
  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • Plans to change fuel types or go all-electric
  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch
  • Venting, relief valve piping, or gas connections that do not look right

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Installing a water heater from scratch pulls in gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines must be sized for the total load on the system and tested for leaks. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits that match their load, sometimes more than an older panel can supply. On the plumbing side, the relief valve needs the right rating and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Check the drain pan and relief valve discharge line now and then for signs of water
