A few minutes of caution while you wait for us can prevent a much bigger mess. For a leak, close the cold supply valve on the pipe above the heater, then cut the power at the breaker on an electric model or turn the gas knob to "off" on a gas model. Move boxes and anything valuable away from the tank and lay towels around the base to slow the spread. Never ignore the smell of gas: leave and call the gas utility from a safe spot.

How Hebron Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
