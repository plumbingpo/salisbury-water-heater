Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Water temperatures that worry you before a health inspection
  • Visible corrosion or water pooling near the tank
  • Higher utility bills that point to a heater losing efficiency
  • Hot water running out during your busiest hour
  • Fixtures that run too hot one minute and cool the next
  • A recirculation loop that no longer seems to do its job
  • Equipment approaching the end of its expected service life

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Keep the area around the heaters clear for combustion air and service access
  • Flush commercial tanks more often than residential ones, often every few months in heavy use or hard water
  • Replace consumed anode rods before the tank itself starts to corrode
  • Budget for replacement as equipment nears the end of its expected life
  • Log water temperatures at key fixtures so drift is caught early
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected
  • Verify delivery temperatures at sinks and showers a few times a year
