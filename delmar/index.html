Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Delmar

Many early failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and is also showing rusty water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

Hot Water Headaches Delmar Homeowners Face

The majority of water heater failures we see in Delmar start with what's in the water. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be a real factor: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the rumbling and popping you may hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

Age is the other big factor. Plenty of homes in the area still have the original tank, and beyond about ten years, the anode rod is usually spent and corrosion starts working on the steel from the inside. The area has a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from failed thermocouples and draft problems to tripped high-limit switches and dead elements.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well can see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless heater in hard water needs annual descaling. And an older install getting a new heater may need updates it never had: an expansion tank, a correctly piped relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. All of these can be fixed, and most are much cheaper when caught early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a quick diagnostic visit can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor. Call (443) 405-4356 and we'll take a look.
